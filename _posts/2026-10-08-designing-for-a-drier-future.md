Planning water services for a changing climate is not only about finding another source of supply. It is also about how water is used, how systems respond to extremes, and how communities can maintain essential services when conditions change.

A resilient approach usually combines several measures rather than relying on a single solution. Demand management can reduce pressure on existing supplies; reuse can make better use of water that has already been treated; and infrastructure planning can consider a wider range of future conditions.

## A portfolio of responses

No option is free of trade-offs. Each measure has different costs, energy requirements, environmental impacts, technical demands and public expectations. The right mix depends on the local context and should be supported by transparent evidence.

## Questions for planners

- Which risks are most important for the service area?
- How much can demand management realistically contribute?
- What treatment and monitoring would reuse require?
- Who benefits from each option, and who bears its costs?
- How will performance be checked as conditions change?

## Resilience is a system property

A robust water future is unlikely to come from one technology alone. It depends on the interaction between infrastructure, operations, institutions, ecosystems and the people who rely on the service.
